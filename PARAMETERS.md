# Parameters of a run

Every parameter of `run(network, demand, transit=None, **parameters)` is a row here: at most 40, each with a unit, a
default, a source and a test that reads it (`tools/check_limits.sh` counts the rows and looks for each name in the
tests). A parameter that is not in this table does not exist.

| Parameter | Unit | Default | Source | Test that reads it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| capacity_per_lane | veh/h per lane | 1 800 | Transportation Research Board, *Highway Capacity Manual*, 7th edition (2022), base saturation flow per lane; a car row without a capacity takes it (a link table's); a network read from OpenStreetMap gives every car link its class's (`osm_class_defaults`) | `crates/graph/tests/network.rs`: `two_lanes_at_1800_veh_h_per_lane_pass_one_vehicle_per_second` |
| bike_speed | km/h | 15 | de Hartog, Boogaard, Nijland and Hoek, *Environmental Health Perspectives* (2011), urban cycling speed in the Netherlands with stops included | `crates/graph/tests/network.rs`: `a_bike_rides_1500_m_at_15_km_h_in_360_s` |
| walk_speed | km/h | 4.8 | Knoblauch, Pietrucha and Nitzburg, *Transportation Research Record* 1538 (1996), measured mean walking speeds | `crates/graph/tests/network.rs`: `a_walker_covers_1000_m_at_4_8_km_h_in_750_s` |
| stop_walk_max | m | 1 500 | El-Geneidy, Grimsrud, Wasfi, Tétreault and Surprenant-Legault, *Transportation* 41 (2014) 193–210: the 85th percentile of the walk to a stop is 524 m for buses and 1 259 m for commuter rail; a trip with no stop within it at either end is offered no journey and is counted (`without_stop` in the iterations table) | `crates/demand/tests/by_hand.rs`: `at_stop_walk_max_1500_m_a_walk_of_1500_m_is_in_and_one_of_1501_m_out`; `crates/loop/tests/by_hand.rs`: `a_trip_1501_m_from_its_stop_is_offered_no_journey_and_is_counted_without_a_stop` |
| transfer_walk_max | m | 400 | the quarter-mile transit walking standard (Guerra, Cervero and Tischler 2012); read once, by the GTFS reader (`crates/io-gtfs`), which bounds the footpaths between stops by it | `crates/io-gtfs/tests/footpaths.rs`: `three_stops_on_a_street_at_4_8_km_h` |
| boarding_slack | s | 60 | GTFS `transfers.txt`, `min_transfer_time` in common feeds; Delling, Pajor and Werneck, *Transportation Science* 49 (2015), the minimum change time | `crates/loop/tests/by_hand.rs`: `with_a_boarding_slack_of_600_s_the_run_at_600_s_cannot_be_boarded_from_a_stop_reached_at_23_s` |
| max_rides | rides | 4 | Dutch journey planners cap at three transfers; RAPTOR rounds beyond four rarely improve the arrival (Delling, Pajor and Werneck 2015) | `crates/loop/tests/by_hand.rs`: `max_rides_1_refuses_a_two_ride_journey_that_max_rides_2_takes` |
| nearby_stops | searches per trip end | 3 | the earliest journey often walks farther than the traveller's cheapest (walking weighs 1.65 times riding), so besides the one search for the earliest journeys between any stops in reach (1: that search alone, beside the cheapest journeys for the class between any stops, which are always searched), each of the next nearest stops of the origin, and of the destination, is tried alone; on the Delft case a journey so added is the cheapest for 19 % of trips at 3, 12 % at 2 and 29 % with every stop tried; of journeys on the same vehicles only the traveller's cheapest is offered | `crates/loop/tests/nearby_stops.rs`: `the_nearest_stop_tried_alone_offers_a_later_journey_with_675_s_less_walking`, `of_two_journeys_on_the_same_run_only_the_cheaper_for_the_traveller_is_offered` |
| choice | name | logit (nests by mode) | the specification: the benchmarks need the deterministic model and the logit family; a run with mode choice needs the logit family | `crates/loop/tests/determinism.rs` (logit), `crates/loop/tests/common/mod.rs` (deterministic) |
| coefficients | per class, sixteen, one per column of the choice row (`crates/loop/README.md`): cost per minute of car driving, bike riding, walking, transit in vehicle, transit access walk, transit egress walk, transit first wait, transit transfer wait, transit transfer walk; per transfer; per €; on ln PS (car routes), minus the exponent of the path size (read times the nest scale under the logit, as 0 under the least cost); per alternative of each mode (car, bike, walk, transit) | 0.1, 0.1, 0.165, 0.1, 0.165, 0.165, 0.145, 0.145, 0.165, 0.5, 0.6, −1, 0, 0, 0.5, 0.25 (one class) | starting values to be calibrated per study; time, walking and waiting weights in the proportions of release 0.1's built-in model; 0.6 per € a value of time of 10 €/h at 0.1 per minute (KiM, *Values of time and reliability in passenger transport*, 2023); −1 on ln PS: the coefficient, its sign turned, is the exponent of the path size in a car route's weight inside its nest, and −1 is the path-size logit (Ben-Akiva and Bierlaire 1999) at every nest scale, in which n identical copies of a route of path size 1/n each count as the one route; the logit divides a whole cost by the nest scale inside a nest, so it reads the coefficient times the nest scale (−0.5 at the default 0.5); the least-cost choice reads it as 0, since the term corrects the logit | `crates/loop/tests/path_size_in_the_car_nest.rs`: `the_logit_reads_the_path_size_coefficient_times_the_nest_scale_and_the_rest_as_given`; `crates/loop/tests/no_congestion.rs`: `car_bike_and_walk_take_their_closed_form_shares_and_their_hand_times`; `crates/loop/tests/choice_row.rs`: `a_journey_with_one_transfer_has_each_part_in_minutes`; `crates/loop/tests/determinism.rs`: `the_same_tables_at_1_and_8_threads_and_over_two_runs` |
| nest_scale | none | 0.5 | Ben-Akiva and Lerman, *Discrete Choice Analysis* (1985), chapter 10: a mid-range nest scale | `crates/loop/tests/determinism.rs`: `the_same_tables_at_1_and_8_threads_and_over_two_runs`; `crates/loop/tests/path_size_in_the_car_nest.rs`: `three_car_routes_and_a_bike_take_the_path_size_logit_in_the_car_nest_by_hand`; `crates/loop/tests/two_journeys_and_a_bike.rs`: `at_the_default_nest_scale_two_journeys_and_a_bike_take_the_nested_logit_shares`, `at_nest_scale_1_two_journeys_and_a_bike_take_the_plain_logit_shares` |
| step_exponent | none | 0.75 | under the logit a traveller holding an alternative is offered a new choice at iteration k with probability 1 / (k + 1)^a (1 is the plain method of successive averages, Sheffi 1985); the averaging settles when the steps sum to infinity and their squares do not (Robbins and Monro, *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 22 (1951) 400–407), for a in (0.5, 1]; a run is refused only for a value that is not a positive finite number; read under the logit only. 0.75 against 1, measured 2026-11-21: on the same choice sets Sioux Falls under the logit (5 seeds) settles at the same mean trip, the two differing by −0.08 ± 0.42 s and +0.03 ± 0.34 s at 300 iterations; at 0.75 the mean trip at iteration 100 is within 0.3 s of its value at 300, at 1 it is 0.8 to 1.2 s above it, and about a third as many travellers would change (0.57 % against 1.62 % at 100 iterations); the test of two car routes behind a bottleneck passes its 20-seed tier at 50 iterations (at 1 it fails, the queue delay rippling by ± 7 s along departure time); Delft stops at 9 iterations instead of 10. The sets grow by the search at the costs each iteration meets, so another value gives other sets and can settle at another state: on Sioux Falls the sets grown at 0.75 settle 8.8 to 8.9 s (1.2 % of the mean trip) above those grown at 1 under either value, in every seed, where the seeds' spread is 0.6 s (1) and 2.1 s (0.75); on Delft the two settle 0.6 s apart on the mean car trip at 60 iterations | `crates/loop/tests/two_routes.rs`: `under_the_logit_at_iteration_1_everyone_is_offered_at_the_plain_step`, `under_the_logit_at_iteration_1_everyone_is_offered_at_the_default_step`; `crates/loop/tests/no_congestion.rs`: `car_bike_and_walk_take_their_closed_form_shares_and_their_hand_times`; `crates/loop/tests/bottleneck_mode_choice.rs`: `after_iteration_1_travellers_would_change_both_ways_and_the_share_is_the_hand_value`, `after_iteration_1_at_the_default_step_the_share_that_would_change_is_the_hand_value`; `crates/loop/tests/by_hand.rs`: `refusals_name_what_they_refuse` |
| iteration_cap | iterations | 50 | below the 60-iteration run test 10 compares the stopped state against | `crates/loop/tests/by_hand.rs`: `a_bike_rides_1500_m_in_360_s_and_a_walker_walks_it_in_1125_s` |
| iteration_floor | iterations | 0 | the convergence tier of tests 4 and 6 runs to a fixed count: the rule stops Sioux Falls at twice the population at 22 iterations where 400 are needed, and the deterministic model's residual at 72 000 travellers vanishes at 1 000 iterations, not at 300 (measured 2026-11-07) | `crates/loop/tests/by_hand.rs`: `the_rule_is_not_read_before_the_floor` |
| link_bin | s | 300 | the usual reporting bin of dynamic assignment; Delft's 2 h window gives 24 bins per link | `crates/loop/tests/two_routes.rs` (60 s bins) |
| seed | none | 0 | any fixed value from 0 to 2⁶³ − 1 (9 223 372 036 854 775 807, the largest integer of the run's record, `run_record.toml`, a TOML file; a larger seed is refused before the run); two runs must agree (test 10) | `crates/loop/tests/determinism.rs`: `the_same_tables_at_1_and_8_threads_and_over_two_runs` |
| osm_class_defaults | km/h, lanes per direction, veh/h per lane | the table in crates/io-osm/README.md | speeds and lanes: version 1's table, 'common OSM speed conventions'; capacities per lane with junctions counted in: 2 000 on motorways (ICTAVRU), 1 800 on trunk roads and 1 550 on ramps (Cerema, *Fondamentaux de la conception routière*), at-grade classes 1 800 × a green share (Cerema 2016, *Évaluation de la capacité aux feux*: Ca = Qs × V/Ty); the green shares by class are this project's assumption, not the guideline's, to be approved by the owner; a sourced national table of speeds is owed | `crates/io-osm/tests/tags.rs`: `the_class_table_reads_as_its_readme_states` |
